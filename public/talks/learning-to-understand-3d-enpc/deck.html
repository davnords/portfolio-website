Primer · the measurement

Structure-from-Motion

SCENE · 6 VIEWS, SPARSE STRUCTURE
X

The scene

A set of 3D points, surfaces, materials. It exists whether or not anyone photographs it.

R,t

The cameras

Each view has a rigid pose and a calibration. Six photographs are six very different questions asked of the same scene.

x

The image

A 2D array of intensities. One dimension of the world has been integrated away, and it is the one we want back.

Decades of research. Deep learning has started to transform it.

Primer · the classical recipe

How the field has solved it for twenty years

1

Detect & describe

Find repeatable points in each image and summarise the appearance around them.

2

Match

Correlate descriptions between a pair of images. Most proposals are wrong.

3

Verify

RANSAC plus a minimal solver. The epipolar constraint discards the outliers and returns R | t.

4

Triangulate

Intersect the surviving rays. The first 3D points in the reconstruction.

5

Register

Add the remaining images one at a time, each against the structure so far.

6

Bundle adjust

Refine every camera and every point jointly, minimising the gap between each observed x and the predicted π(K[R|t]X).

And nothing is learned, each scene is solved from scratch. The points and cameras contain no higher order features.

Matching is a crucial step. All the subsequent steps depend on matching keypoints.

Part two · the landscape

Three common ways of “learning” 3D

3D lives in the solver
3D lives in the weights
(i)  OPTIMISATION / GEOMETRY

Solve it per scene

COLMAP, GLOMAP, + learned priors
Objective

Reprojection error, per scene, from scratch.

What is learned

Nothing persists. “Learning” = the reconstruction itself.

Limit

No representation beyond cameras and points.

(ii)  FEED-FORWARD RECONSTRUCTION

Amortise the solver

DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT, π3
Objective

Supervised regression onto 3D pseudo-labels.

What is learned

A fast approximation of (i), plus latents useful downstream.

Limit

A data problem and a weak learning signal.

(iii)  SINGLE-VIEW SSL

Hope 3D falls out

DINO → DINOv2 → DINOv3, MAE, iBOT
Objective

Self-distillation within one image.

What is learned

The strongest general-purpose features in vision.

Limit

The objective never compares two views.

Note the dependency. (ii) learns from (i)'s output.

Paradigm (ii)

Feed-forward reconstruction

Amortised, meaning that the per-scene optimisation is paid for once, during training, and absorbed into weights. Unlike (i), this one leaves something behind: latents now being used well outside reconstruction.

But trained on what?

On paradigm (i)'s output: COLMAP and sensors supply every pointmap it regresses onto.

The first paradigm that leaves a model behind. Which raises the next question: what is it learning from?

Paradigm (ii)

Two problems with feed-forward reconstruction

Problem 1 · the data

Where COLMAP fails, on textureless or repetitive scenes and with few views, there is no label. The failure is inherited, and the training set is bounded by the labeller.

TWO ATTEMPTED ESCAPES · SYNTHETIC DATA AT SCALE (VGGT) · AN SSL OBJECTIVE BOLTED ON (VGGT-Ω)

Problem 2 · the learning signal

A depth map is largely predictable from one image. A pose is a handful of scalars. Neither target forces the network to relate two views to each other, which is the part that is actually hard.

SO EVEN WITH PERFECT LABELS, TRAINING FROM SCRATCH IS SLOW AND UNSTABLE

Both problems point the same way. If the signal matters more than the labels, why keep paying for the labels?

Paradigm (iii)

Self-distillation, in one picture

Two ways of looking at one image. The teacher gets the large global crops whole. The student gets them with half the patches masked out, plus small local crops, so it has to recognise the scene from a fragment.

The loss is agreement. Both heads emit a distribution over 65k prototypes; the student is trained to match the teacher. The teacher is never trained. It is an EMA of the student, so nothing outside the model supervises anything.

Then freeze it. A linear probe on those features beats task-specific supervised training on segmentation, depth, classification and retrieval, all off one backbone. And it now sits upstream of paradigm (ii): VGGT's encoder is DINO.

Caron et al. DINO, ICCV 2021  ·  Oquab et al. DINOv2  ·  Siméoni et al. DINOv3  ·  Zhou et al. iBOT

This is the strongest result in representation learning. And every crop in it came from one photograph.

SE(3) structure can be read out of 2D features. Many of you saw Maks Ovsjanikov present this.

It is remarkable how much 3D leaks into a model that only ever sees one image.

But leaking is not learning. If the probing community keeps finding faint 3D structure in 2D models, the obvious move is to stop probing for it and put it in the objective.

The probing literature keeps measuring the same quantity. How much 3D survives an objective that was never about 3D.

02
Part three · the argument

The problem
with one view

The issue is not that single-view learning is weak in practice. The information is not in the input.

The argument

The argument is a hundred and twenty years old

A motionless observer
cannot acquire
the notion of space.

HENRI POINCARÉ  La Valeur de la Science, 1905  ·  paraphrased

Space is not given by the senses. It is constructed from the correlation between how you move and how what you see changes. A second view is exactly that motion, and it is the one thing a single-image objective is structurally guaranteed never to get.

We did not find this argument ourselves. Chen et al., SeeSE3, the paper Maks Ovsjanikov presented here, is what put Poincaré in front of us, and their SE(3) protocol is the one we borrow later.
Our method is named after him for that reason.

Multi-view consistency is a free, unlabelled signal that is actually about 3D. The dominant SSL paradigm cannot use it.

Part four · what came before

The lineage Poincar3 comes out of

Two things move from left to right: how many views the objective sees, and what it is asked to predict. Everything here is self-supervised. The disagreement is about the target.

Poincar3 is the far corner of both axes: many views, and no pixels in the objective at all.

Why the target changed

Completion is ill-posed wherever the two views do not overlap. Nothing in view B says what is behind the occluder in view A. A covisibility label is defined everywhere.

Weinzaepfel et al. CroCo, NeurIPS 2022  ·  Loiseau, Bourmaud & Lepetit Alligat0r, NeurIPS 2025 spotlight  ·  Nordström, Edstedt, Kahl & Bökman MuM, CVPR 2026

The label is free. Eight views of one scene and a 3D point that all of them see. The reconstruction already knows where it lands in every frame, so the track costs nothing.

Poincar3 · what fails

First, the objective that does not survive from scratch

VGGT-Ω's self-supervised objective is an MSE loss in feature space between student and teacher. On top of a model that is already strong, it refines. Starting from random weights, it has nothing to hold it up.

A regression loss in feature space exerts no pressure towards structure. Nothing stops the representation drifting somewhere smooth, low-rank and useless.

The same budget, the same architecture, the same data. Only the objective changed.

Poincar3 · method

What stops it collapsing

TEACHER ASSIGNMENTS · ROWS ARE THE BATCH, COLUMNS ARE PROTOTYPES · press to iterate

The teacher outputs a distribution over 65,536 prototypes. Nothing in the loss says those prototypes have to be used.

The failure mode

Raw softmax piles the mass onto a handful of prototypes. Every view gets the same answer, the student matches it perfectly, and the loss is satisfied by saying nothing.

Sinkhorn–Knopp, three iterations

Normalise the columns and every prototype receives equal total mass. Normalise the rows and every sample is a distribution again. Alternate three times.

DINOv2 ALSO USES SINKHORN–KNOPP PLUS KOLEO TO PREVENT COLLAPSE

Collapse is the default behaviour. Self-distillation works only because something external forces the targets to stay spread out.

Outlook · the thread

One thing kept showing up in all three

“Correspondence, correspondence, and correspondence.” Takeo Kanade, on the three most important problems in computer vision

The only learned stage. Everything else is solved mathematics, and nothing downstream recovers from a bad correspondence. LoMa · +18.6 mAA

Nobody trained it to. Linear-probe matching beats DINO, and attention knockout destroys epipolar geometry. RoMa-Ω · Bratulić et al.

And it transfers. 94.9 PCK@50 by attention alone, above models trained with 3D labels, and the ones that match best transfer best. Poincar3

Correspondence is the operation that turns images into geometry. All three paradigms keep rediscovering it.

Outlook

Three candidates for the convergent objective

Candidate A

Video generation

  • The closest thing vision has to next-token prediction.
  • Sees motion parallax, so multi-view is in the input for free.
  • Geometry stays implicit and non-metric.
  • Enormous compute for a signal mostly about appearance.
Candidate B

Feed-forward reconstruction

  • Outputs exactly the quantity we want, in one pass.
  • Already the practical default for sparse-view 3D.
  • Bounded by the labeller, and the signal is weak.
  • Better as a head than as a pretraining objective.
Candidate C

Multi-view SSL

  • The only loss here that is both label-free and geometric.
  • Inherits the DINO lineage and adds the missing constraint.
  • Composes with B as the initialisation that makes it work.
  • No DINOv2-scale demonstration yet. My bet is here.

No convergence yet. 3D vision is somewhere around pre-2018, and the objective is still unclaimed.

Outlook

What I do not know

Q1

Should we just be using video models?

Much simpler and scales very well.

Q2

Why do we need all these tricks and engineering?

Language understanding seems to "just work" without any tricks.

Q3

Where does language fit in?

Perhaps language can be combined with 3D vision to stabilize the self-supervision.

Q4

Why are vision encoders so small in LLMs?

Trillion parameter LLMs use vision encoders that are a few hundred million parameters.

Vision is far from solved. We are all waiting for a simple pre-training objective. Or, video models are already the answer but I refuse to believe it.
